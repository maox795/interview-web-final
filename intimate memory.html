Childhood Intimate Memories


Interviewer: Hi, Mary. I'm so glad to have you here. Recently, I've been thinking about a question: Why do we keep things? About half a year ago, I moved and found a bunch of useless stuff in my house, like souvenirs and random objects. It made me wonder whether we keep things because they're useful or simply because we can't let them go. Is there something you used as a child that you still keep today? Do you use it, or do you just keep it somewhere?

Mary: I have a blanket I used as a child. I keep it, but actually, my mom is the one who takes good care of it.


Interviewer: So your mom keeps it because it reminds her of you when you were little?

Mary: Yes. She wants to keep some of the things I used when I was little because she thinks they're meaningful.


Interviewer: That's really sweet. I don't have anything from when I was a baby that's still with me. What does your blanket look like?

Mary: It's blue and has little rabbits on it.


Interviewer: That's cute.

Interviewer: Have you ever thought about throwing it away? Does your mom ever take it out and touch it, maybe to feel the texture?

Mary: I've never thought about throwing it away.


Interviewer: What if it suddenly disappeared one day? How would you and your mom feel?

Mary: I think we would both feel sad if the blanket disappeared.


Interviewer: Sometimes, the memory behind an object matters more than the object itself. A blanket is an ordinary thing that everyone has, but yours is important because it was used on you when you were a child. Even if the memory stays with us, why do we still need the object?

Mary: The person who gave me the object is very precious to me. My blanket was given to me by my mom, so I want to keep it because of her, no matter what memories I have.


Interviewer: That makes sense. Sometimes, we need a physical object to attach our emotions to. When we miss someone or a certain feeling, we can touch something tangible. At what age do you think an ordinary object starts to become more than just a thing?

Mary: I think when I was about seven or eight years old, because that's when I started being able to remember things that happened in my life.
