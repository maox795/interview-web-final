Why Do People Keep Things


Interviewer: Have you ever thrown away something that you wish you had treasured or kept?

Mary: I've never actually thrown away any of my own objects. Many things were thrown away by my mom when I was too young to tell her that I wanted to keep them, so I feel a little regret about that.


Interviewer: Can you give me an example of something your mom threw away?

Mary: After we moved to another city, some of my childhood picture books and illustrations were moved to other places by my mom. I still have some of them, but others were given to the children of our relatives.


Interviewer: Do you think there might be a time in the future when you suddenly feel able to let go of something you've kept for a long time?

Mary: Maybe when I feel that it no longer has any value to me, or when I no longer have a connection with the person it came from.


Interviewer: Is keeping things a rational decision for you, or is it more of a gut feeling? Do you decide whether to keep something, or do you feel compelled to keep it?

Mary: I think it's more of a compulsion. I always feel that if I don't keep something, I might regret it someday.


Interviewer: Do you sometimes take these things out to look at them, or do you just leave them around your house?

Mary: I often look through them.


Interviewer: What do you think people are really holding on to when they keep things? Is it a fear of forgetting, a sense of security, or something else?

Mary: I think people follow their hearts. There are many reasons why people keep things. They might like something, think it's valuable, or keep it because it was given to them by someone precious. These things can represent a part of their lives. Everyone has different experiences and stories.
